research

Mixed-signal ICs and system architectures for neural interfaces, FPGA-based sensor systems, and high-speed wireline links.

We study mixed-signal integrated circuits and the system architectures around them, where sensing front-ends, data conversion, clocking, and digital processing are treated as one design problem. Current work follows three connected directions, pursued through university research and industry-linked sensor and readout projects.

  • Neural Interfaces
  • FPGA-based Systems
  • High-speed Interfaces

Neural Interfaces

An implantable neural interface must record microvolt-level signals across many channels and deliver precise stimulation, all within tight power, area, and wireless-bandwidth budgets. We study these trade-offs at the architecture level, from acquisition and on-chip data reduction to stimulation and wireless links. Within the Brain-to-X research direction of Korea's Alchemist Project, we contribute circuit-level work as one member of a multi-institution team.

  • Low-noise recording front-ends and ADCs with spike-aware on-chip data reduction
  • Programmable multi-site current stimulation with charge-balanced pulse delivery
  • Wireless power delivery and bidirectional data links matched to the implant's energy budget

FPGA-based Systems

Custom sensors and readout ICs generate wide, continuous data streams that must be captured with precise timing and processed with predictable latency. We study FPGA architectures for this problem: sensor timing and synchronization, parallel streaming data paths, and the division of processing between programmable logic and host software.

  • Timing, synchronization, and configuration architectures for sensor and ASIC control
  • Parallel capture and streaming data paths with configurable signal processing
  • Hardware–software partitioning between programmable logic and host-side processing

High-speed Interfaces

Clock jitter, channel loss, and timing skew constrain the bandwidth and energy efficiency of high-speed chip-to-chip links. We study transmitter, receiver, and clocking architectures for serial links, treating the channel's nonidealities as part of the design problem.

  • Transmitters: serializers and differential output drivers
  • Clocking: low-jitter clock generation, distribution, and clock and data recovery
  • Receivers: equalization (CTLE/DFE), sampling, and timing alignment
